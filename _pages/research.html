Fracture in Living Tissues

How do living tissues fail, and what determines where a fracture starts and how it propagates?

We study fracture and spontaneous rupture in epithelial tissues, focusing on how cell mechanics, tissue topology and collective activity control the nucleation and propagation of cracks and holes. We use cell-based theoretical approaches, including vertex and multi-phase-field models, to connect mechanics at the scale of individual cells with tissue-scale failure.

Relevant publications

  • D. E. P. Pinto, Jan Rozman, and Julia M. Yeomans, Multi-phase field model reveals internal dissipation is crucial for spontaneous hole formation in cell monolayers, Nature Communications 17, 8288 (2026). [paper]

Deformable Particles under External Flow

How does external flow organize dense collections of soft, deformable particles?

We investigate the collective dynamics of dense suspensions of deformable particles, where particle deformation, interactions and topological rearrangements are strongly coupled. Using multi-phase-field simulations, we study how an external flow profile generates positional and orientational order and can produce collective, self-sustained shape oscillations. We also develop simplified dynamical models to identify the mechanisms linking microscopic rearrangements to macroscopic material behaviour.

Relevant publications

  • I. Hadjifrangiskou, R. N. Valani, and D. E. P. Pinto, Shear-Induced Collective Shape Oscillations in Dense Soft Suspensions, arXiv preprint arXiv:2602.08445 (2026). [paper]

SAT-Assembly and Folding

Can we design simple building blocks that reliably assemble or fold into complex structures?

We use inverse design and Boolean satisfiability (SAT) to design interactions between building blocks so that a desired structure is allowed to assemble while competing structures are excluded. We apply this framework to the self-assembly of finite three-dimensional structures and to the self-folding of 3D shells, using computational models to connect interaction design with the resulting structures and assembly pathways.

Collective Dynamics of Cells and Tissues

How do interactions between individual cells give rise to collective tissue behaviour?

We use cell-based models and statistical-physics approaches to understand how cellular mechanics, motility and the environment determine the collective dynamics of dense tissues. Our work addresses questions including the transition between solid-like and fluid-like behaviour, the structure of the tissue energy landscape, and how substrate disorder and spatial patterns influence cell motion and tissue organization.
